About the data

Where the numbers come from

Every price on Bulk Board is copied from a store's own public "bulk rates", "buylist" or "sell to us" page. Each store's row links to the exact page it came from. We only list a price a store has published; if a store buys bulk but asks you to contact them for a quote, it is listed under the table as "doesn't publish rates".

How prices are compared

  • Everything is converted to the same unit. Stores quote per card, per 100, per 1,000, or per box. Bulk columns (commons, energy, basic lands, unsorted) are shown as dollars per 1,000 cards; everything else as dollars per card.
  • Cash first. The default view shows only money you can actually spend elsewhere (PayPal, check, Venmo, etc.). Switch to "Cash or store credit" to include store-credit rates, which are usually higher but only useful if you shop there.
  • Outside the US stores show their own currency, with a US-dollar estimate underneath using European Central Bank exchange rates refreshed weekly. Shipping bulk across a border rarely pays, which is why they are hidden by default.
  • Store categories don't always line up. One shop's "holo rares" may include V cards, another's may not. Each store's own wording is kept: hover a price, or click the store, to see exactly what it said.
  • When a store has more than one price for a column (for example, a higher rate for cards sorted by set), the table shows the highest and marks it +. Click the store to see the conditions.

Marketplace buyers

About 150 of the buyers come from TCG Bulk, a marketplace where individual collectors and small shops post what they pay per card type. You sell to them through a free TCG Bulk account; TCG Bulk takes a 10% fee from the seller, so their prices here are shown after that fee. Each row shows how many orders the buyer has completed and, when known, how much of their monthly budget is left. Their prices are refreshed automatically every week. Only the buyer's display name and state are shown here.

How it stays current

Every Monday an automatic check downloads each store's rate page and compares every dollar amount on it with last week's. If the amounts match, the store's date moves forward (✓). If any amount changed, the store gets a ⚠ page changed flag until a person re-reads the page and updates the table. Pages that need a full web browser to show their prices can't be checked automatically and age visibly instead — a date older than 45 days turns amber.

Pack value page

The Pack value page uses TCGplayer prices for sealed products, published daily by TCGCSV. For each product it works out how many packs are inside, then the price per pack and how that compares with buying the same packs one at a time in that set. The single-pack price is the middle of that set's booster-pack listings.

  • Pack counts are read from the product's own description when it states one ("from listing"). Otherwise the usual contents for that kind of product are used ("usual count"), for example 36 packs in a Pokémon booster box, or 9 in a Scarlet & Violet–era Elite Trainer Box. Products whose contents can't be worked out are left out rather than guessed.
  • Market price is what a product has recently sold for; cheapest listing is the lowest asking price right now, from one seller, before shipping.
  • Extras aren't valued. Promo cards, sleeves, dice and storage boxes are listed but not counted, so a product that costs a little more per pack may still be the better buy.
  • Bulk Board isn't affiliated with or endorsed by TCGplayer.

Open or sell: how pack value is estimated

  • Pull rates (how often each rarity shows up) come from the publisher where they publish them (Wizards does for Magic), otherwise from large community opening studies. Each set shows where its rates came from and how confident they are.
  • Within a rarity, every card is assumed equally likely. That's the standard simplification; real print runs sometimes differ.
  • Market value adds up every card at its TCGplayer market price. If you sell takes off 13% in selling fees and $0.50 handling per card sold, and counts any card that would net less than a bulk buyer pays at that bulk price (from the Bulk rates table).
  • A 5,000-pack simulation per set gives the spread: the typical (median) pack, the top 10%, and the chance one pack beats its price. Because a few chase cards carry most of the value, the typical pack is usually worth much less than the average.

Before you ship

Rates change, especially after new sets release. Stores count and grade your cards when they arrive and can pay less than the posted rate for miscounts, damage, or cards they don't want. Always confirm the current rate, minimum and shipping rules with the store before you send anything.

Help keep it accurate

Know a store that's missing, or a price that's wrong? Suggest a store or report a wrong rate. The full data set is free to reuse: buyers.json.
