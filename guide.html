Getting the most for bulk

Figures are from October 2026: US buyers, cash payouts, marketplace fees already taken off. The rates table always has the current numbers.

The short version

  • Plain commons barely pay for postage and your time. A thousand Pokémon commons weigh about 4 lb and usually sell for $11–23. Shipping them across the country can cost half of that.
  • The money is in the higher tiers. One ex or V card typically pays 30–55¢, as much as 20–50 commons. Sort those out first.
  • Where you send it matters as much as what you send. Buyers' prices for the same category routinely differ by 2–3×. Sorting the table by a column takes one click; use the calculator before you ship.

Sort before you sell

  1. Pull anything worth real money first. A free scanner app (the TCGplayer app, ManaBox for Magic, Collectr) prices a card in a second. Anything over about 25¢ is worth more as a single than as bulk. In Pokémon these are mostly reverse holos of popular Pokémon and recently played trainers; TCGplayer publishes a monthly list of the most valuable "bulk" Pokémon cards.
  2. Then split by tier: ex/V-class, full arts and illustration rares, holos and reverse holos, rares, then commons and uncommons. Every tier above commons is priced per card, and each one is worth more than a stack of commons.
  3. Take out what nobody wants: basic energy, damaged cards, and (for many buyers) older yellow-border Pokémon or Magic sets older than five years. Read each buyer's "doesn't buy" list.

What shipping really costs

  • About 4 lb per 1,000 cards. A full 5,000-count box is about 22 lb.
  • USPS Ground Advantage, bought at discounted label prices (Pirate Ship, eBay, Shippo, etc.) from the East Coast, comes to about $7 per 1,000 cards to the Midwest and $12 to the West Coast. At the post office counter it's $11–18 per 1,000. (USPS Notice 123, Oct 2026.)
  • Stay at or under 20 lb per box. Prices jump at 21 lb and again at 23 lb.
  • For long distances, Priority Mail flat-rate boxes can be cheaper. A Large flat-rate box ($32.75 at label prices) holds roughly 4,000–4,500 loose, rubber-banded cards. A 5,000-count storage box doesn't fit in any flat-rate box. Ask the buyer whether they accept loose bundles.
  • Media Mail is not allowed for trading cards.
  • Some buyers pay shipping or send a prepaid label above a certain size. That can be worth more than a higher posted rate.

Picking a buyer

  • Cash or store credit? Credit usually pays 20–30% more, but it's only worth it if you'll spend it there.
  • Read the sorting rules. Some buyers charge a 20–25% fee or send the box back (at your cost) if it isn't sorted their way.
  • Send a test box first (1,000–2,000 cards) before shipping 10,000 to a buyer you haven't used. Photograph what you send and keep the tracking number.
  • Spread large lots across two to four buyers. Buyers drop categories when they have enough, and a single buyer can pause without warning.
  • Marketplace buyers (TCG Bulk) are individuals and small shops. TCG Bulk charges the buyer's card when you add tracking and takes a 10% fee from you (already deducted in the table). Check how many orders a buyer has completed and how much of their monthly budget is left. A buyer with $40 left can't pay for your 10,000 cards.
  • Reputation lives in forum threads and reviews. Search the store's name plus "bulk" on Reddit or Elite Fourum before sending a large box.

Buying bulk to flip: the math

Unsorted Pokémon bulk from individuals usually goes for $10–20 per 1,000. Packaged 1,000-card lots on eBay ask $25–60, which is more than any bulk buyer pays, so those are retail, not a source.

A worked example: you buy 10,000 unsorted Pokémon cards for $200 ($20 per 1,000) and spend about 16 hours sorting them. A realistic mix sells to bulk buyers for about $190, plus about $70 from singles you pick out and sell yourself. Take off about $50 of shipping and $25 of supplies and fuel, and you roughly break even before paying yourself anything.

What changes the outcome, biggest first:

  1. The price you pay. Every $10 per 1,000 is $100 on a 10,000-card lot. Below about $10 per 1,000, it starts to pay.
  2. The singles you pull. A handful of good cards can be worth more than all the commons. Sample 200 cards before you buy a big lot.
  3. Shipping. Selling to a nearby buyer, or in person, removes the biggest cost.
  4. How many ex/V-class and holo cards the lot holds. Each ex-class card is worth about 30 commons.

Bulk is a volume-and-sorting business with thin margins. It works best as a side effect of buying collections for their good cards, where the bulk is the leftover you clear out, not the thing you're paying for.

Before you ship

  • Re-check the store's live rate page; prices on this site can be up to a week old.
  • Confirm the minimum, who pays shipping, and how they want the cards packed.
  • Count carefully: most buyers recount and pay for what they find.
